PROVENANCE & EDITORIAL METHOD

Show the working.

A public technical series adapted from the Infosec view of a persistent Notion journal.

What supplied the material

Twenty-three security-related records were inspected from a view containing thirty-two entries. The original technical notes and resource lists supplied the themes. Coding and game entries outside this series were not reviewed for publication.

What changed in editing

The articles group related notes by topic, replace informal shorthand with explanations, and link primary references beside factual claims. Exercises and templates are new editorial additions. The material does not claim professional engagement results, completed labs, or security certification.

Blank records contribute no claims. Image-only material was not transcribed; the Application Hacking entry supplies only its topic. Original screenshots, private comments, exam scores, and credential material are not included. Source dates describe the notebook history, not proof of technical currency.

Technical corrections

  • SQL values should be parameterized; “sanitize the query bar” is too vague.
  • XSS and CSRF cross different boundaries and require different controls.
  • DNSSEC validates signed DNS data; it does not encrypt queries.
  • Kerberos V5 does not require public-key cryptography for every session.
  • The Heartbleed advisory supplied a fix in April 2014; old uncertainty about a patch was not carried forward.

Each article links the relevant primary documentation. Sources were checked during preparation on 30 October 2026. This series is a curated snapshot; it has no live connection to the private notebook.
